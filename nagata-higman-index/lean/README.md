# The Nagata–Higman index is at most n² − 2 for n ≥ 3 — the Lean 4 proof

[![Lean build](https://github.com/david44hello/papers_supporting_materials/actions/workflows/lean_build.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/david44hello/papers_supporting_materials/actions/workflows/lean_build.yml)

Author: Dave Goel. Copyright (C) Dave Goel 2026--now. All rights reserved.

This folder is a formal proof, checked by the Lean 4 proof assistant, of every numbered result of the paper
"Improving Razmyslov's (1974) bound: the Nagata–Higman index is at most n² − 2 for n ≥ 3" by Dave Goel, with the named
steps of its proofs, its remarks and its worked examples. Lean checked version 1.04 of the paper. The paper's link will
be added here when it is posted.

## The theorems, in plain words

Over a field F of characteristic 0, let d(n) be the Nagata–Higman index: the least m such that every associative
(not necessarily unital) algebra in which every element x has xⁿ = 0 also has x₁x₂⋯x_m = 0 for all its elements. Razmyslov (1974) proved
d(n) ≤ n²; Kuzmin (1975) conjectured d(n) = n(n+1)/2. The paper proves:

- **Theorem A:** for n ≥ 2, d(n) ≤ n² − 1.
- **Theorem B:** for n ≥ 3, d(n) ≤ n² − 2.
- **Corollary 1.1:** for n ≥ 3 and every k ≥ 1, the polynomial invariants of k-tuples of n × n matrices over F under
  simultaneous conjugation are generated by the traces tr(X_{i₁} ⋯ X_{i_r}) with 1 ≤ r ≤ n² − 2 (from Theorem B and the
  cited theorem of Procesi and Formanek).

Theorems A and B are proved in two forms (shown here for Theorem B; Theorem A is the same with n² − 1): in the free algebra, the
product x₁x₂⋯x_{n²−2} of the first n² − 2 letters lies in the ideal generated by all n-th powers; and in algebra form,
every product of n² − 2 elements of an algebra with xⁿ = 0 for all x is 0. In Lean:

```lean
theorem NagataHigman.Paper.thm_B (n : ℕ) (hn : 3 ≤ n) :
    firstLettersProd F (n ^ 2 - 2) ∈ TIdeal F n ∧ nhIndex F n ≤ n ^ 2 - 2
```

## What the proof assumes

Mathlib, Lean's three standard axioms, and three published results that the paper cites, stated in
[NagataHigman/Cited/Classical.lean](NagataHigman/Cited/Classical.lean) and
[NagataHigman/Cited/MatrixInvariants.lean](NagataHigman/Cited/MatrixInvariants.lean):

| Cited result | Source | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `youngSystem_exists`: the classical representation theory of the symmetric group S_N in characteristic 0 (the irreducible representations, Fourier inversion, the Young basis with Jucys–Murphy eigenvalues, the sign twist) | James, LNM 682 (1978), Theorems 4.12 and 6.7; Ceccherini-Silberstein–Scarabotti–Tolli (2010), Chapter 1; Jucys (1974); Okounkov–Vershik (1996) | Theorem A, Proposition 2.1 (index form), (F0), (F2), Remarks 2.3 and 4.4 |
| `youngSystemC_exists`: the same over ℂ, with the splitting of S^λ over the alternating group (James–Kerber 2.5.7) and the part of Frobenius' theorem that says the two characters agree off the class h(λ) (Theorem 2.5.13) | James–Kerber (1981) | Theorem B, Lemma 4.3, d(6) ≤ 34, Corollary 1.1 |
| `procesi_formanek`: the invariants of any number of n × n matrices under simultaneous conjugation are generated by traces of products of degree at most d(n), over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 | Procesi, Adv. Math. 19 (1976), Theorem 3.3; Formanek, LNM 1197 (1986), Theorem 6; as stated in Drensky (1995), Theorems 5.3 and 5.6 | Corollary 1.1 only (Lean proves the step from an algebraically closed field to every field of characteristic 0) |

Each cited statement is no stronger than its source. The first two were also tested by exact computation on small
cases (every N ≤ 6, and every self-conjugate shape with N ≤ 9):
[scripts/modelcheck_youngsystem.py](scripts/modelcheck_youngsystem.py) and [scripts/altsplit_check.py](scripts/altsplit_check.py),
with their logs. The third is stated from Drensky's Theorems 5.3 and 5.6, which the Lean file quotes word for word.
Theorem B uses exactly one of them, `youngSystemC_exists`, besides Lean's three standard axioms (row 107 of
[AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md), which `scripts/check.sh` regenerates from Lean's `#print axioms`).

## Where each claim of the paper is

[NagataHigman/Paper/Statements.lean](NagataHigman/Paper/Statements.lean) has one Lean theorem per claim, named by the
paper's numbering: 114 claims, covering every numbered result, the named steps inside the proofs, the remarks and the
worked examples. [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) lists them all with their paper lines: 98 PROVED (from Lean's standard axioms
alone), 16 PROVED-FROM-CITED (from the standard axioms and the cited results named in that row). Lean checks a proof
of every statement, so a referee checks only that the definitions ([NagataHigman/Defs/](NagataHigman/Defs/)), the cited results
([NagataHigman/Cited/](NagataHigman/Cited/)) and the statements say what the paper says.

## What is not formalized

- Sentences that only report other papers' results (the history in Section 1, the known values d(4) = 10 and
  d(5) ≤ 21) are not formalized.
- In 10 places Lean proves a step by another argument than the paper's text (for example, where the paper uses the
  branching rule (F5), Burnside's theorem (F6) or the last sentence of (T1), Lean proves the consequence that the proof
  needs); [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) lists them under "Route differences", and `scripts/route_check.lean` checks the list.
- Line numbers in Paper/Statements.lean, Defs/ and Cited/ refer to the LaTeX source of version 1.04 of the paper; a few
  comments (in Proofs/ and in Defs/FirstReturn.lean) cite an earlier draft and say so ("v1.02 line ...").

## How to check it yourself

Prerequisites: the `elan` Lean toolchain manager, Git, curl, Python 3 and ripgrep (`rg`). From a fresh copy of this
repository:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/david44hello/papers_supporting_materials.git
cd papers_supporting_materials/nagata-higman-index/lean
git checkout nagata-higman-index-v1.04   # the version the paper cites
lake exe cache get      # downloads prebuilt Mathlib files (it does not compile Mathlib)
lake build
bash scripts/check.sh   # build, scan for proof holes (sorry, admit, native_decide, ...), regenerate AUDIT.md
git diff --exit-code AUDIT.md   # the regenerated AUDIT.md is identical to the published one
```

GitHub runs these same steps on a fresh machine at every change (the "Lean build" mark at the top; the steps are in
[.github/workflows/lean_build.yml](../../.github/workflows/lean_build.yml) at the top of this repository).

Optional, after a build: `scripts/kernel_replay.sh` re-checks every declaration with Lean's kernel and with independent
proof checkers (it needs the checker binaries listed at the top of the script). Checkers that refuse any project with
cited assumptions report SKIPPED, by design.

Versions: Lean 4.35.0 (pinned in `lean-toolchain`); Mathlib v4.35.0, commit `56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679`
(all dependencies pinned in `lake-manifest.json`).
